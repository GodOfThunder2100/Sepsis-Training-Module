      <!-- STEP 1: SEPSIS DEFINITION -->
      <section class="panel active" data-panel="0">
        <h2>What is Sepsis?</h2>
        
        <div data-role-content="clinical">
          <div class="definition-box highlight-border">
            <h3>Clinical Definition (Sepsis-3 Consensus)</h3>
            <p class="definition-text">
              <strong>Sepsis</strong> is defined as a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. In frail aged-care residents, acute deterioration in organ function is quantified by a rapid change in baseline parameters (such as qSOFA or NEWS2 triggers).
            </p>
            <p class="definition-text">
              <strong>Septic Shock</strong> occurs when cellular/metabolic abnormalities deepen, characterized by persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP &ge; 65 mmHg and serum lactate &gt; 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div data-role-content="support">
          <div class="definition-box highlight-border">
            <h3>Simple Definition</h3>
            <p class="definition-text">
              <strong>Sepsis is a medical emergency.</strong> It happens when the body's immune system overreacts to an infection (like a chest or urinary infection) and starts harming its own organs and tissues instead of just fighting the germs.
            </p>
            <p class="definition-text">
              Without quick treatment, sepsis can turn into <strong>septic shock</strong>, causing organs to fail fast. In older adults, it can happen in a matter of hours.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <h3>Visual Progression & Presentation</h3>
        <p class="lede">Sepsis causes systemic tissue hypoperfusion. Patients often look acutely toxic, lethargic, or profoundly mottled.</p>

        <div class="media-gallery">
          <div class="media-card">
            <img src="https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/sites/default/files/styles/embedded/public/2026-05/sepsis-hub-3.png.webp?itok=HT_w0nRj" alt="ACSQHC Clinical Care Standard - Could it be Sepsis?" class="gallery-img" />

      <!-- STEP 2: SIGNS & SYMPTOMS -->
      <section class="panel" data-panel="1">
        <h2>Signs &amp; symptoms</h2>
        <p class="lede">Sepsis in older adults rarely looks textbook. Fever is often absent — watch for change from baseline instead.</p>

        <div class="card-grid">
          <div class="info-card">
            <h3>General signs</h3>
            <ul>
              <li>Fast breathing or shortness of breath</li>
              <li>Fast heart rate</li>
              <li>Fever <em>or</em> feeling abnormally cold</li>
              <li>Clammy, mottled or discoloured skin</li>
              <li>Extreme pain or physical discomfort</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
          <div class="info-card info-card--flagged">
            <h3>In older adults specifically</h3>
            <ul>
              <li>New or worsening confusion — often the <em>first</em> sign</li>
              <li>Sudden functional decline — uncharacteristic weakness</li>
              <li>Reduced appetite or fluid intake</li>
              <li>Increased falls or unsteadiness</li>
              <li>"Just not themselves" — a critical red flag</li>
            </ul>
            <p class="citation">Wester et al., 2013; Clifford et al., 2016</p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="callout" data-role-visible="clinical">
          <strong>Clinical Note:</strong> Diagnostic error rates exceed 10% in older patients due to diagnostic overshadowing from existing comorbidities.
        </div>

        <div class="callout" data-role-visible="support">
          <strong>Support Note:</strong> You don't need to diagnose anything. Notice the change and report it immediately.
        </div>
      </section>

      <!-- STEP 3: ESCALATION -->
      <section class="panel" data-panel="2">
        <div data-role-content="clinical">
          <h2>Recognise thresholds, escalate with ISBAR</h2>
          <p class="lede">Aged-care-derived early warning triggers require rapid escalation.</p>

          <div class="vitals-grid">
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Resp. rate</span><span class="vital-value">&gt;22 <small>or</small> &lt;8</span></div>
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Heart rate</span><span class="vital-value">&gt;100</span></div>
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Temperature</span><span class="vital-value">&lt;36&deg; <small>or</small> &gt;38&deg;</span></div>
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Systolic BP</span><span class="vital-value">&lt;100</span></div>
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Confusion</span><span class="vital-value">New / worse</span></div>
            <div class="vital"><span class="vital-label">Urine output</span><span class="vital-value">Reduced</span></div>
          </div>

          <div class="isbar">
            <h3>Escalate with ISBAR</h3>
            <div class="isbar-row"><span class="isbar-letter">I</span><div><strong>Identify</strong> — yourself, resident, location</div></div>
            <div class="isbar-row"><span class="isbar-letter">S</span><div><strong>Situation</strong> — immediate concern</div></div>
            <div class="isbar-row"><span class="isbar-letter">B</span><div><strong>Background</strong> — relevant history</div></div>
            <div class="isbar-row"><span class="isbar-letter">A</span><div><strong>Assessment</strong> — vitals, clinical picture</div></div>
            <div class="isbar-row"><span class="isbar-letter">R</span><div><strong>Recommendation</strong> — action required & timeframe</div></div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div data-role-content="support">
          <h2>How to inform the nurse</h2>
          <p class="lede">You are the front-line warning system. Staffing ratios mean your observations save lives.</p>

          <div class="say-this">
            <h3>When alerting the nurse, state:</h3>
            <ol>
              <li><strong>Who</strong> — resident name & room</li>
              <li><strong>What</strong> — exact change observed</li>
              <li><strong>How different</strong> — compared to baseline</li>
              <li><strong>Context</strong> — recent falls, missed meals</li>
            </ol>
          </div>
        </div>
      </section>
